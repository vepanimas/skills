# Prompt wording

Copy a stem, the universal template, or the worked example into the prompt. Replace `[topic]` or `[task]` with the user's subject. Do not rewrite the rest. The adaptation rule for a non-JTBD comparative primer is in `SKILL.md`.

## Stems

### Quickly understand an unfamiliar topic

Structured primer

"Give me a structured primer on [topic], covering the fundamental concepts, terminology, mental models, and practical examples."

### Explore different approaches or schools of thought

Comparative overview

"Provide a comparative overview of the major schools of thought on [topic], highlighting their core principles, differences, strengths, and limitations."

Use this stem when they asked for a comparative analysis or comparative overview on its own. The worked example below is the heavier combination.

### Learn how to apply something in practice

Practical field guide

"Create a practical field guide to [topic], focusing on real-world applications, decision-making frameworks, common pitfalls, and worked examples."

### Learn a repeatable process

Actionable playbook

"Develop an actionable playbook for [task], including step-by-step workflows, templates, checklists, and examples."

### Create a reusable reference

Cheat sheet

"Create a concise, information-dense cheat sheet for [topic], optimized for quick reference during day-to-day work."

### Understand complex nuances

Deep dive

"Give me an in-depth treatment of [topic], exploring the underlying reasoning, subtle distinctions, trade-offs, and advanced applications."

## Universal template

Use this for a product discipline when no narrower format was named. Product disciplines are product management, UX, user research, product analytics, and strategy.

Give me a **structured, information-dense primer** on [topic], covering the fundamental concepts, mental models, major approaches, and practical applications.

Use concise explanations and short, realistic examples. Include common pitfalls, rules of thumb, and actionable takeaways.

Prioritize conceptual clarity, practical applicability, and depth of understanding over exhaustive coverage.

Finish with a **practical cheat sheet** I can use as a quick reference in my day-to-day product work.

## Worked example

Comparative primer + field guide + cheat sheet, for schools of thought the user will apply. Copy this request as written when the topic is Jobs-to-Be-Done.

Create a **comprehensive yet concise comparative primer** on the major schools of thought within Jobs-to-Be-Done (JTBD), accompanied by a **practical field guide and cheat sheet** for product managers.

**Learning objectives:**

- Understand the major JTBD approaches, their origins, fundamental principles, and key differences.
- Learn their core concepts, terminology, frameworks, and methodologies.
- Understand when and why to apply each approach in real product work.
- Develop enough practical understanding to start applying JTBD independently.

**Content requirements:**

1. **Big-picture overview:** Introduce the JTBD landscape and explain how the major schools relate to one another.
2. **Comparative breakdown:** For each school, explain its core philosophy, underlying assumptions, main concepts, and methodology.
3. **Key concepts:** Define essential terms in plain language, with short, concrete examples.
4. **Practical application:** Explain when to use each approach, with realistic examples from software products and developer tools.
5. **Trade-offs and pitfalls:** Highlight limitations, common misconceptions, and mistakes practitioners make.
6. **Comparison:** Include a side-by-side comparison of the approaches, emphasizing their differences and appropriate use cases.
7. **Quick reference:** Finish with a compact, actionable cheat sheet summarizing the key frameworks, questions, techniques, and decision rules.

**Writing guidelines:**

- Prioritize conceptual clarity and practical applicability over academic depth.
- Be information-dense but easy to skim.
- Use short illustrative examples rather than lengthy case studies.
- Introduce essential terminology without unnecessary jargon.
- Use structured tables and checklists where helpful.
- Distinguish established concepts from your own interpretations.
- Reference authoritative sources and original authors where appropriate.
